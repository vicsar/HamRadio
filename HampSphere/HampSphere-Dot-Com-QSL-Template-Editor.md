# hamsphere.com QSL Template Editor

## BEFORE YOU BEGIN

### What the QSL Template editor really is

- The QSL Template editor is a tool that allows you to design a custom QSL card to send to another station after a radio contact (QSO). You can personalize the card with your own text, images, and other design elements.
- The preview button at [https://hs50.hamsphere.com/qsledit.php](https://hs50.hamsphere.com/qsledit.php) does not show a live comment you type somewhere. Instead, it displays a fixed placeholder example ("TNX FER QSO"), which is classic amateur radio shorthand for "Thanks for the contact."
    - This shorthand comes from the early days of Morse code (CW), when operators shortened words to tap out messages faster:  
    **TNX**: Phonetic shorthand for "Thanks."  
    **FER**: Phonetic shorthand for "for" (in Morse code, E is a single dot . and O is three dashes ---, making FER faster to send than FOR).  
    **QSO**: Standard international radio Q-code for a 2-way radio contact or conversation between two stations.  
- That means this feature is designed for individual contacts so you can add a custom comment to your QSL card. I n HamSphere, a QSL card is represented by a custom 495x315 pixel digital card that officially confirms a 2-way radio contact (QSO) between two stations. The name comes from the standard radio Q-code "QSL," which means "I confirm receipt of your transmission."
  
> **Please DO NOT send a QSL Card unless you have had a QSO (made contact) with a station!**
  
## How to use the comment feature

### Part 1: Configure and Save the QSL Template
1. In the QSL Template Editor, position red box `13` on the card, wherever you want the custom comment to appear (place any other you might need).
2. (Optional) Double-click any box to adjust its font size or color.
3. Verify that Boxes `5` through `9` and box `12` are dragged onto your card image.
> Note: If these boxes are missing, the card will not display the receiving station's callsign, frequency, or signal report. 
4. Click SAVE.

### Part 2: Add a Comment and Send the QSL Card

1. Open the HamSphere radio application or web transceiver.
2. Click Cluster/Log, then select Log Book.
> This guide assumes you have already contacted another station and logged the interaction. I repeat, **DO NOT** send a QSL Card unless you have had a QSO (made contact) with a station!
3. Find the contact (QSO) you want to confirm and click the gray letter `S` on the right side of that row.
4. Select your saved QSL card template.
5. Enter your custom message into the `Comment` text box.
6. Click `Preview` to verify how your text appears in position 13
7. Click `Send`.

### So, why do operators use QSL cards? 
- Earning Awards & Diplomas: Exchanging QSL cards officially verifies a contact on a one-on-one basis, which you need to qualify for HamSphere awards, contest certificates, and diplomas. 
- Checking Equipment Performance: By looking at the signal report (RST) alongside the power and antenna details written in the comment section, operators can tell how well their radio and antenna setup is performing.
- Collecting Souvenirs! Just like real-world ham radio operators collect paper QSL postcards from around the globe, HamSphere operators design creative cards and collect them from different countries, islands, and special events.
